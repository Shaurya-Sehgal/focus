CAMPAIGN PLAN
Planned targets by January 19, 2027: four presentations reaching approximately 100 students; 500-1,000 accounts reached online; 50 eligible challenge registrations; at least 30 completions. These are goals, not achieved results.
The secondary evaluation target is a 15% reduction in average daily use of selected social-media apps, subject to approvals and technical feasibility.
Four planned classroom sessions last 15-20 minutes each, combining a local statistic, a short activity, a demonstration of built-in usage settings and a practical habit.
Planned publicity includes ten QR-code posters, two educational social posts weekly, school announcements and student-council reposts.

CHALLENGE
Planned baseline week: January 4-10, 2027.
Planned challenge week: January 11-17, 2027.
Participants record daily minutes for a fixed list of selected social-media apps, using the same list and measurement method across both weeks. Whole-app time can include messaging and longer videos; it does not isolate short-form video.
Example habits already in the campaign: a phone-free study block or avoiding short-form entertainment during the hour before bed.
Submitted award proposal: logging the chosen habit on at least five of seven days and submitting a short reflection earns one prize-draw entry. A five-question learning quiz earns one bonus entry. Winners are drawn randomly. Participation is voluntary and free.
Final rules, eligibility, required consent, approved data handling and any prizes must be confirmed before opening registration. No prizes or external contributions are currently confirmed by this brief.

PROPOSED TECHNOLOGY
The website provides educational resources and, later, registration information. The proposed app provides instructions, reminders, personal goals, habit logs and learning prompts. Automatic tracking, device support and app distribution are unconfirmed. The informational website does not monitor phone usage.
Required school approvals and consent precede recruitment and data collection. Future evaluation uses participant codes and published group summaries, reporting attendance, reach, registrations, completions, quiz scores and usage separately. Before/after changes alone do not establish causation.
